# Synthetic data

**Synthetic data** is data that imitates the characteristics and patterns of real-world data but is produced by
algorithms, generative models or simulations instead of being written or recorded directly by people.[^s4] In modern
AI it most often means text, code, dialogues or preference labels generated by one language model and used to train,
align or evaluate another model, or the same one. The usual motivations are scarcity of real data, privacy
constraints, and the cost of collecting and annotating it by hand.[^s4]

## Origins and two traditions

The idea predates large language models. Statistical agencies have long released synthetic versions of
confidential datasets: NIST's 2023 guidance on de-identifying government data distinguishes *partially synthetic*
data, where risky cells in real records are replaced with imputed or noised values, from *fully synthetic* data,
where a model is fitted to the original records and entirely new records are simulated from it.[^s6] NIST notes
that only formal methods such as differential privacy give fully synthetic data a quantifiable privacy guarantee,
and that a synthetic dataset can never carry more information than the model that produced it.[^s6]

The machine-learning tradition is about capability rather than disclosure control. A landmark example is
**Self-Instruct** (Wang et al., ACL 2023), which bootstraps an instruction-following dataset from a model's own
outputs: starting from a small pool of seed tasks, GPT-3 generates new instructions, inputs and outputs; invalid or
near-duplicate samples are filtered out; and the original model is fine-tuned on the result.[^s1] The authors
reported a 33% absolute gain over vanilla GPT-3 on Super-NaturalInstructions, roughly matching InstructGPT-001,
which had been trained on private user data and human annotations.[^s1]

## How it is used

Synthetic data now appears at every stage of the LLM pipeline. In pretraining, Microsoft's phi-1 (2023) showed that
data quality can substitute for scale: a 1.3B-parameter code model trained on about 6B tokens of filtered web code,
under 1B tokens of GPT-3.5-generated "textbooks" and about 180M tokens of synthetic exercises reached, as reported by
its authors, 50.6% pass@1 on HumanEval and 55.5% on MBPP.[^s2] In alignment, NVIDIA reported in June 2024 that
over 98% of the data used to align Nemotron-4 340B was synthetically generated, and released a reward model and
permissive licence partly so others could build similar generation pipelines.[^s5] A 2024 Google DeepMind–led
survey catalogues further uses: math and code reasoning traces, simulated tool-use and planning trajectories,
rendered image–text pairs for multimodal models, back-translation for multilingual tasks, AI-feedback preference
labels, and synthetic test sets and red-teaming prompts for evaluation.[^s4]

A typical recipe has three steps: *generate* candidates with a strong model (often guided by seed examples,
personas or templates), *filter* them with heuristics, deduplication, reward models or execution checks such as
unit tests, and *mix* the survivors with real data before training.[^s1][^s4][^s5]

## Risks and limitations

- **Model collapse.** Shumailov et al. (Nature, 2024) showed that when generative models are trained recursively on
  their predecessors' output, they first lose the tails of the original distribution and eventually converge to a
  low-variance distribution that barely resembles it. They attribute this to compounding statistical-approximation,
  expressivity and learning-procedure errors.[^s3] In their OPT-125m experiments, keeping 10% of the original human
  data in each generation largely prevented the degradation.[^s3]
- **Fidelity and hallucination.** Generated examples can be fluent but wrong, and models trained on them may fail
  to generalise to real inputs.[^s4]
- **Bias amplification.** Generators can reproduce or magnify the biases of the model that produced them.[^s4]
- **Evaluation contamination.** Paraphrased benchmark items in synthetic corpora can evade token-level
  decontamination, which is why the DeepMind survey urges developers to keep protected in-house benchmarks.[^s4]
  The phi-1 authors likewise had to argue at length that their synthetic exercises had not leaked HumanEval.[^s2]
- **Privacy is not automatic.** NIST cautions that synthetic data without formal guarantees offers no provable
  protection against re-identification.[^s6]

## Connections

Synthetic data sits under **pretraining** and **fine-tuning** as a source of training signal for both.[^s2][^s1]
It is closely tied to **knowledge distillation**, since generating data with a large "teacher" model and training a
smaller "student" on it is a sequence-level form of distillation, and to **instruction tuning**, which Self-Instruct
made cheap.[^s1] Synthetic preference data and AI-generated feedback increasingly supplement human labels in
**RLHF**-style alignment.[^s4][^s5] Its main failure modes link it to **model collapse**, to **benchmark
contamination** in **AI evaluation**, and to **algorithmic bias**.[^s3][^s4] On the privacy side it is a common
vehicle for **differential privacy**, which can make a synthetic release provably protective.[^s6]
