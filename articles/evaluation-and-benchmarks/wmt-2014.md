# WMT 2014

WMT 2014 (often written WMT14 or WMT'14) refers to the shared tasks of the Ninth Workshop on Statistical Machine Translation, held with ACL 2014 in Baltimore on 26–27 June 2014.[^s2] Its news translation task released training corpora and fresh test sets, called newstest2014, for French, German, Czech, Russian and Hindi paired with English in both directions.[^s2][^s1] Although the workshop itself centred on statistical systems, two of its language pairs, English-German and English-French, became the default yardstick for the neural machine translation models that followed, including the sequence-to-sequence LSTM, attention-based encoder-decoders and the Transformer.[^s3][^s4][^s5]

## Construction of the task

The organisers supplied parallel training data including Europarl v7, News Commentary v9, a Common Crawl corpus, and, for French-English only, the UN corpus and the 10⁹ French-English corpus; Czech, Russian and Hindi had their own resources such as CzEng 1.0, Yandex 1M and HindEnCorp.[^s2] Monolingual news crawl and Gigaword data were also allowed. Participants were mainly asked for "constrained" systems trained only on this data, so that comparisons would reflect methods rather than data access; systems using substantial extra data had to be flagged.[^s2] Hindi-English was run as an experimental low-resource pair.[^s2]

The test sets were built from online newspaper articles. About 1,500 English sentences were professionally translated into each other language, and about 1,500 sentences originally in each other language were translated into English, giving roughly 3,000 sentences per pair (3,003 for most pairs, 2,507 for Hindi-English).[^s1] newstest2013 was suggested as the development set.[^s2]

## Results and evaluation

The findings paper reports 143 submitted systems from 23 institutions across the ten translation directions.[^s1] Automatic metrics such as BLEU and TER were reported, but the official ranking came from human judgement: annotators ranked five anonymised system outputs per source sentence, and those rankings were converted into pairwise comparisons.[^s2][^s1] The organisers compared three ways of turning these into a ranking and adopted TrueSkill, which predicted held-out judgements best; about 328,830 pairwise judgements were collected, from researchers and trusted community members rather than crowd workers.[^s1] Alongside translation, the workshop ran quality estimation, metrics and medical translation tasks.[^s1]

## Becoming the neural MT benchmark

Neural systems adopted WMT 2014 data almost immediately. Sutskever, Vinyals and Le reported 34.8 BLEU on the WMT'14 English-to-French test set with a deep LSTM, against 33.3 for a phrase-based statistical baseline, and 36.5 when the LSTM reranked that baseline's candidates (NIPS 2014).[^s3] Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio trained their attention model on a data-selected subset of about 348 million words drawn from the roughly 850 million words of WMT'14 English-French data, testing on the 3,003-sentence news-test-2014 (ICLR 2015).[^s4]

The Transformer paper made the benchmark canonical: it trained on about 4.5 million WMT 2014 English-German sentence pairs and the larger 36-million-sentence English-French set, used newstest2013 for development and newstest2014 for testing, and reported 28.4 and 41.8 BLEU respectively for its big model (NeurIPS 2017, authors' own figures).[^s5] For years afterwards, "WMT14 En-De" and "WMT14 En-Fr" BLEU served as headline numbers for new sequence architectures.

## Limitations

Comparisons on WMT 2014 are more fragile than a single number suggests. Matt Post showed that BLEU is a parameterised metric whose value depends on tokenisation and reference normalisation, with differences of up to 1.8 points between common configurations; he recommended WMT's own scoring scheme and released SacreBLEU to standardise reporting (WMT 2018).[^s6] Papers also preprocess the same nominal dataset differently, for example in vocabulary size and data selection, which complicates apples-to-apples comparison.[^s4][^s5] The test sets are also narrow: about 3,000 sentences per pair, all drawn from news articles of a single year, so results say little about other domains such as conversation or technical text.[^s1]

## Connections

WMT 2014 is a benchmark within **AI evaluation** and **machine translation** (`broader`).[^s1] It was **introduced in** the WMT 2014 findings paper and is one edition (**partOf**) of the annual WMT shared-task series, now the Conference on Machine Translation.[^s1][^s6] It is **used in** the evaluations of **Attention Is All You Need** and of Bahdanau et al.'s alignment paper, and in **sequence-to-sequence learning**, which links it to the **Transformer architecture**.[^s3][^s4][^s5] Its automatic scores rely on **BLEU**, while its official rankings illustrate **human evaluation** at scale.[^s1][^s6]
